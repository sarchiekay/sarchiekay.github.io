My Story
📸 Add your photo here:
assets/sarah.jpg

I studied environmental science some time back at UCT - not exactly the typical route into tech. I fell into a startup, The Greenest Office, as a Client Liaison (if we ever meet, I'll tell you how that happened - it involved two lads - a Brit and a Kiwi, both twice my age - and tequilas in an English pub in Cape Town. I ended up working with them for 7 years). I was good at it. And somewhere between the customer calls and the support tickets, I realised I had more questions about the product than answers. Turns out being a natural planner with an eye for detail, a habit of asking too many questions, and a need to understand how things actually work - is quite useful when your job is to make sure nothing gets missed.

That curiosity pulled me into product management. I built the discipline over four years of full product ownership - compliance, time and attendance, health and safety, and checklist platforms - digitising paper-based processes across mobile and web. In my last year I also took on a concurrent role as Lead Product Owner at Gearly, a startup spun up by the same founders, where we digitised document signing and workflow management. Then Deimos Cloud, where I contracted across multiple clients and learned to earn trust fast in rooms I had never been in before. Every engagement was different. Getting up to speed quickly and making good decisions without full context - that skill has stayed with me.

Now fintech. And the work took on a different weight. I grew up in Africa. I understand - not abstractly, but personally - what it means when financial services were not built with you in mind. Building credit products, digital wallets, and payments for people who have been historically underserved is not just a job I am good at. It is work I believe in.

Along the way I have pushed into territory most POs have the luxury to leave to engineers - drawing UML sequence diagrams, reading API contracts, mapping system flows, and supporting my BAs with the business analysis that keeps requirements sharp. A lot of this has come from necessity. In several roles I have not had a technical lead to lean on, which meant steering technical conversations myself and collaborating directly with architects to find solutions. I have filled those gaps through online courses, self-directed learning, and a lot of asking questions, not always the right ones, but enough to get outcomes that matter.

What I Do

What working with me actually looks like.

I'm not the PO who writes stories and disappears into a Jira board. Here's what you actually get.

⚡

Product Execution

User stories with business context, acceptance criteria QA can test independently, and specs that are clear for devs. The result: sprints that ship on time, ruthless prioritisation, and concessions that allow for iterative development while considering the customer, business value, and technical inputs.

📦

Delivery Management

Backlog ownership, sprint planning, and coordination across engineering, support, compliance, and ops. I own the delivery end-to-end and coordinate any operational complexity so the team stays focused on shipping. Predictable delivery, not because it's simple, but because the details are handled.

📐

Technical Authorship

I step into the technical seam when there's no technical lead: reading API contracts, mapping system architecture, understanding how pieces fit together. I ask the questions that surface the requirements engineering teams need but didn't know to spec. Not a developer, but fluent enough in systems to support the team when building requirements that reflect technical reality.

🛡️

Product Operations

I own the product from dev, testing to launch through steady state: support training, SOP creation, in-country UAT, compliance sign-offs, reconciliation testing. Not because I do all the work, but because someone needs to own the whole thread and ensure nothing slips. If it touches the product, I'm accountable for it.

I'm certified in Scrum PO, Scrum Master, and PRINCE2 Agile. The frameworks have been the foundation that help me support the team to ship, iterate and improve. I'm fluent in Agile, and I have learned that it works best when it serves the team, not the other way around. Scrum must work for us. Not us for Scrum.
